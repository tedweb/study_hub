Percent Change & Exponent Rules

Multiplying by 1.07 for +7% · fractional exponents & radicals

Fixes these misses: M1 Q17 (percent), M1 Q16 (fractional exponents).

Core concepts

Percent change as a multiplier

  • Increase by p% → multiply by (1 + p/100). A 7% increase → ×1.07.
  • Decrease by p% → multiply by (1 − p/100). A 7% decrease → ×0.93.
  • Successive changes multiply: +10% then +20% → ×1.10×1.20 = ×1.32 (not +30%).

Exponent rules

  • xa·xb = xa+b  ·  xa/xb = xa−b  ·  (xa)b = xab.
  • (xy)a = xaya.

Fractional exponents & radicals

x(1/n) = n√x , x(a/b) = b√(xa)
  • Rewrite radicals as fractional exponents to combine them cleanly.
  • 7√(x9y9) = (x9y9)(1/7) = x(9/7)y(9/7).

Worked example — a clean percent multiplier

Problem. City A’s population rose 7% from 2015 to 2016. If 2016 = k × 2015, what is k?
A 7% increase means multiply by 1 + 0.07 = 1.07.
So k = 1.07 (this is the M1 Q17 answer).
Watch out: "+7%" is ×1.07, not ×0.07 and not ×1.7. This is a one-line idea but it cost a point on M1 Q17. For exponents, rewrite every radical as a fractional power before combining — don’t guess.
